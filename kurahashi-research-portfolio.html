Research Brief — Kurahashi Island Shipbuilding Heritage

Sample
倉橋島 Kurahashi Island: Preliminary Research Summary
Prepared for genealogical and historical book project · Focus: Shipbuilding families, Hiroshima Prefecture

Administrative Context

Kurahashi Island (倉橋島) is located south of Kure City in Hiroshima Prefecture. Formerly the town of Kurahashi (倉橋町, Aki District), it was incorporated into Kure City in March 2005. Any historical records prior to 2005 may be filed under Kurahashi-chō or Aki-gun administrative records.

Shipbuilding Background

The island has a long tradition of wooden shipbuilding (木造船建造) dating back several centuries. Its proximity to Kure — home to the Imperial Japanese Navy's largest naval arsenal (呉海軍工廠) — made the surrounding area a center of maritime industry through the Meiji, Taishō, and Shōwa periods.

Key Record Types to Request

  • 戸籍謄本 / 除籍謄本 (family register excerpts)
  • 住民票の写し (residence records)
  • 土地台帳 (land registry records)
  • 地域史・町史 (local history publications)
  • 職業別名簿 (occupational directories)

Recommended Initial Contacts

  • 呉市役所 倉橋支所 (Kurahashi branch of Kure City Hall)
  • 呉市立中央図書館 (Kure City Central Library)
  • 呉市海事歴史科学館 (Yamato Museum)
  • 広島県立図書館 (Hiroshima Prefectural Library)
  • 国立国会図書館 デジタルコレクション
Research Note Administrative records for Kurahashi Island prior to the 2005 municipal merger are held under former Kurahashi-chō (倉橋町) records. Requests to Kure City Hall's Kurahashi branch (倉橋支所) should specify the pre-merger period to ensure correct routing. The National Diet Library Digital Collections (国立国会図書館デジタルコレクション) contains digitized regional histories and period directories accessible remotely.

To: Local Materials Section, Kure City Central Library

Dear Library Staff, I hope this message finds you well. I am writing on behalf of a Japanese-American researcher based in the United States, for whom I am providing research assistance as part of a book project. I would be grateful if you could advise us on the following: · Holdings of local history materials related to Kurahashi Island's shipbuilding and regional industries · Availability of occupational directories or local chronicles from the former Kurahashi-chō period · Procedures for accessing materials, and whether remote copy requests are accepted We understand you are busy and we deeply appreciate your time. We look forward to your kind reply. Respectfully yours, Erika Hashimoto (Research Assistant, Osaka, Japan)

Source — Japanese

瀬戸内海は、古来より日本の海上交通の要衝として栄えてきた。数百もの島々が点在するこの内海では、船は単なる移動手段ではなく、人々の生活と産業を支える根幹であった。

とりわけ安芸国（現・広島県）の島嶼部においては、豊富な木材資源と熟練した船大工の技術が相まって、独自の造船文化が育まれた。倉橋島をはじめとする島々では、世代を超えて受け継がれた木造船の建造技術が、地域経済の中心を担っていた。

明治期以降、近代化の波とともに鉄船の需要が高まるなかでも、伝統的な木造船技術はその価値を失わず、漁船や小型の運搬船として広く用いられ続けた。

※ This source text is an original sample composed for this portfolio. It does not reproduce any published or copyrighted material.
Translation — English

The Seto Inland Sea has long served as a vital artery of maritime trade in Japan. Among its hundreds of scattered islands, boats were far more than a means of transport — they were the foundation upon which people's livelihoods and industries depended.

In the island communities of Aki Province (present-day Hiroshima Prefecture) in particular, abundant timber resources combined with the refined skills of local ship carpenters to nurture a distinctive shipbuilding culture. On islands such as Kurahashi, the craft of wooden boat construction — passed down across generations — stood at the heart of the regional economy.

Even as the Meiji period brought modernization and growing demand for iron vessels, traditional wooden boatbuilding retained its relevance. Fishing boats and small cargo carriers built by local craftspeople continued to be widely used throughout the era.
